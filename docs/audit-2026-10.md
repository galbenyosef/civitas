# Civitas against the city-builder field: audit, October 2026

Written 2026-10-18 from the `main` branch at `03fe71f` (Education E2), read-only. It builds on `docs/feature-gaps.md`
(2026-10-16) and `docs/early-game-audit.md` (2026-10-17) and says what has changed since. Every Civitas claim names a
file or a doc; every claim about another game cites a numbered source in section 9. Numbers measured here were
measured on a machine shared with other agents, so treat them as ±20%. Ground truth for the tree as audited:
`npm run typecheck` passes (35 s, including `local:check`) and `npm run demo:check` passes (9,167 buildings, 12 lines,
3 s).

**The yardstick.** Rich's stated goal is "a comparable-level entry game which is fun". This audit therefore weighs
everything by what a newcomer meets in the first one to three hours against the entry-level experience of the leading
city builders, not by breadth. Section 5 is that bar in one place; the roadmap in section 8 is ordered by it.

What changed since `feature-gaps.md` was written two days ago: multi-tile structures, sub-kinds, a 97-kind catalogue
with 582 designs, size ladders, merged lots and block interiors, bridges, roundabouts and flyovers, gateways and
migration, lives (ageing, households, health, happiness), leisure venues, buses, rail with freight and level crossings,
airports, helicopters, the metro (U1-U8), boats and harbours (B1-B8), mobile jobs, the world map with estates, charts,
onboarding goals, the ledger, local/offline mode with PWA, spectator mode and the public showcase, the London demo, the
Education data model and institutions (E1-E2). That is most of the feature-gaps "next" list, built in 48 hours. The
list of what it did **not** touch is the spine of this audit: land value, pollution, utilities, incidents, loans and
budgets, road classes, terrain, parcel trading, accounts, audio, and anything a player does after hour two.

## 1. Executive summary

**Where Civitas stands.** Civitas is a technically serious, unusually deep *simulation* wrapped in a thin *game*. On the
things that are hardest to build it is already at or past the commercial field: every citizen is a named agent with a
home, job, shift, household, health, friends, car and commute; traffic is lane-accurate and collision-free with real
junction conflicts; buses, trains, tube trains, ferries, planes, helicopters and boats all run as pure functions of the
clock so they cost the wire nothing; you can ride in any of them from the driver's seat; it runs in a browser, offline
as a PWA, or as one shared persistent world. No shipped city builder lets you click a pedestrian, read her week's
money, and sit in her taxi.

Measured against "a fun, comparable entry-level game", it is not there yet. A newcomer to Cities: Skylines sees houses
rise within a minute at 3× speed, gets a milestone and a new toy every ten minutes, hears the city, is told by an icon
when a building has no power, and runs the game on a 2015 laptop. A newcomer to Civitas online waits 10-12 real
minutes for a first resident with no way to speed time up (pause and speed exist only in local mode,
`client/src/local/ui.ts`), is guided well for an hour by 13 goals, then has nothing left to aim at; nothing can go
wrong, nothing makes a sound, problems are found by hovering, and no one has measured the client on an ordinary laptop.
The shared world, which is the strategic bet, had 1,901 citizens on day 425 and zero clients when probed (UAT
`/healthz`); the public site is a read-only London demo.

**Three biggest strengths**

1. **The agent simulation and its legibility.** Lives, shifts, car ownership, parking, carpools, mode choice, mobile
   jobs, migration through gateways, all per person and all inspectable (`sim/life.ts`, `sim/carpool.ts`,
   `sim/mobile.ts`, `sim/gateways.ts`). Cities: Skylines II promised "fully realised" individual simulation and shipped
   an economy players called fake [5][7]; Civitas actually does it, at 60k citizens inside a 50 ms tick on one Node
   process (`docs/load-tests.md`). This is also the entry-level hook nobody else has: "follow one person's day".
2. **Transport as a first-class system.** Bus laps that turn round, rail with freight and barriers, a twin-bore metro
   with a Tube-map view, ferries, bascule bridges, airports with six plane types, helipads, and cab, cockpit and
   wheelhouse first-person views (`docs/transit.md`, `docs/rail.md`, `docs/underground.md`, `docs/boats.md`,
   `docs/airport.md`). Transport Fever is the only reference with comparable breadth and it has no citizens [18].
3. **Delivery machinery.** A data-driven catalogue with AI design packs and a turntable viewer, a showcase that
   `demo:check` keeps complete, bot-driven gauntlets, A/B traffic harnesses, load tests, local and spectator modes
   from one code base, a UAT lane. This is why 48 hours produced what it produced, and it is the asset to protect.

**Five biggest gaps, weighed for a new player**

1. **The first ten minutes are dead and the hour after that cannot bite.** First resident at 10-12 minutes, cranes with
   nobody on them, no time control online, then a city in which nothing can go wrong: no fires, crime, outbreaks,
   bankruptcy; stations send vehicles to random buildings (`simulation.ts:2811`); money goes negative for ever
   (`docs/start-from-zero.md` "Failure: none"). The only tension is a traffic jam the player cannot fix
   (`docs/early-game-audit.md` friction 3).
2. **Nothing to aim at after hour one.** 13 goals end at "First bus line" at minute 60-80; 37 tech nodes are then a
   shop. No milestones, ranks, scenarios, events or win states. Every reference keeps handing the entry-level player a
   next thing for 10-20 hours [1][20][32].
3. **Problems are invisible and the data views are thin.** One overlay (congestion), no icons over buildings that are
   stuck, unstaffed or full, 30+ single-letter shortcuts that cannot be remapped, click accuracy and road looks that
   the owner himself flagged (UTD 96efc16d, d08904bb). CS1's info views and CS2's icon language are the entry-level
   baseline [3][27].
4. **The economy is a head count.** Income is about $4 a resident a day; no land value, rent, goods, budgets, loans or
   land market; land is $10 a tile anywhere (`shared/src/constants.ts`). There is nothing for a new player to
   optimise and no reason a park or a school pays back.
5. **Genre table stakes: sound, utilities, polish on an ordinary machine, accounts.** Zero audio code
   (`grep AudioContext` finds nothing); no power or water; client performance measured only on an M2 Max with Metal
   (`docs/load-tests.md`); identity is the name you type (`net.ts:206`) and anyone may bulldoze a public road
   (`net.ts:508`).

**Verdict.** The simulation is a moat; the game around it is a prototype. The next quarter should spend less on new
vehicle classes and more on the first three hours: a start that responds in seconds, a city that can fail, a ladder
that lasts, feedback you can see and hear. Breadth can wait; every reference game that launched broad and shallow
(Cities XXL, Highrise City) was punished for it, and every one that launched narrow and fun (Townscaper, Mini
Motorways, Islanders) sold [22][42][43][47].

## 2. Positioning

**What Civitas is.** A browser-native, server-authoritative, unbounded city builder where players own land parcels
in one persistent world and the citizens belong to nobody (`README.md` "The core idea"). Things nobody else ships
together:

| Angle | Civitas | Nearest comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agent-level simulation you can read | every person has a name, age, household, health, job, shift, car, taste, happiness, week's money, friends (`sim/life.ts`, `CitizenInfo` in `shared/src/protocol.ts`) | CS1/CS2 cims have names and a destination; SC13's GlassBox agents took the nearest home and job and were criticised for it [12][31] |
| Shared persistent world with land ownership | parcels, adjacency and no-islanding rules (`shared/src/landrules.ts`), per-parcel tax, estates, taxes flowing between owners | SC13 regions (separate cities, shared services, Great Works) [13]; CS multiplayer only via the CSM mod, still "in development" [4]; Eco's governance sandbox [38]; no commercially successful persistent shared-world city builder exists [13][38][39] |
| First-person everywhere | walk as a citizen, drive, ride a bus, train cab, tube cab, cockpit, wheelhouse (`client/src/render/fpv.ts`, `tunnelRide.ts`, `instruments.ts`) | TF2's passenger camera; Pocket City 2's avatar walk [18][47] |
| Browser, offline, PWA, spectator, one build | `docs/local-mode.md`, `SPECTATE=1` (`docs/demo.md`) | TheoTown and Pocket City are mobile; micropolisJS is a 1989 port; nothing comparable runs an authoritative sim in a Web Worker [47] |
| Pure-function timetables | planes, trains, metro, boats placed by `(tiles, tick)` on both sides, zero per-vehicle wire (`shared/src/rail.ts`, `flights.ts`, `boats.ts`) | TF2 and CS run vehicles as entities |

**The audience it could own.** The genre is mainstream again: three of Steam's five most-wishlisted games in October
2023 were city builders, Manor Lords sold a million copies in a day, and CS2's troubled launch and the November 2025
exit of its developer left room that W&R, Manor Lords, Foundation and Anno 117 have each taken a slice of
[5][19][21][23][24][28][33]. Civitas should not chase the CS2 crowd, who want 500k-pop painted cities, 10,000 workshop
assets and a GPU to match [25]. Its natural audience is three overlapping groups: people who liked *watching* SimCity
2013 and CS1 more than optimising them ("follow one person through her day"); transport enthusiasts (the Transport
Fever and Mini Metro crowd, who get timetables, cab rides and freight here); and small groups of friends who want a
persistent shared town the way they share a Minecraft server. The browser removes the install barrier and the
spectator mode is a trailer that runs in a tab. For the entry-level goal the first group matters most: they want a
city that visibly responds, a next thing to do, and a story they can tell about it.

## 4. Major gaps, ranked by impact on a new player

Sizes: S days, M about a week, L multi-week, XL re-architecture (agent-time, as in `feature-gaps.md`).

### 4.1 The first ten minutes are dead (M)

*Why it matters.* Entry-level players judge a builder in the first session. CS1 shows the first houses within a minute
at 3× speed; Townscaper and Islanders respond to every click instantly, which is why they sold [22]; Anno 117
loosened its needs so newcomers advance sooner [33]. A dead opening is the single most common reason a free browser
game is closed.

*What exists.* The 13-goal chain is good: the first four goals fall in the first minute. Then the card waits: first
resident at 10-12 real minutes because a starter yard's crew begins with the next day shift and a house is 60 units of
work (`early-game-audit.md` "What is still open"); 96 lots of cranes with nobody on them read as broken (friction 1,
partly fixed by the starter yard). Online there is no pause or speed; a game day is 8 real minutes and `SIM_STEPS`
is a server-wide env var. Joining at night was black; now a hint (friction 10).

*Fix.* Settlers and builders at once: the pioneer camp from `start-from-zero.md` B1 (opens at once, ~8 beds, its
people take the handyman jobs), a first house that takes a third of the work, and a newcomer gate that releases its
first cars within one game hour. Measure with the gauntlet bot at every join hour.

### 4.2 Nothing can go wrong: no consequence loop (L)

*Why it matters.* Every durable city builder is a tension between growth and the things growth breaks. CS1's fires,
crime, deaths and garbage piles; SC13's agent fire trucks and crime waves; Frostpunk built a genre out of "something
is about to go wrong" and its sequel's council of factions is what reviewers single out [20][35]. CS2 players
attacked exactly the absence of this: goods that did not move and money that did not bite were called a "fake
economy", and Colossal Order spent its first year on an Economy 2.0 rewrite [7]. Players stop when nothing resists
them.

*What exists.* Stations give homes a coverage score that scales growth ×1.2 per service and raises tax tolerance by 5
points (`simulation.ts:817`, `:2888`); with Patrols they send vehicles to random buildings (`simulation.ts:2811`).
Health is real per person (`sim/life.ts`) but illness only costs shifts. Money can go negative with no effect except
refused builds. Happiness lowers leaving odds and that is all.

*Fix.* "Incidents: fires, crimes and medical calls with real targets" (UTD 22143abc) with "separate fire, police,
health" (751c35a0): coverage becomes response time, buildings visibly burn, crime cuts venue takings, the sick need a
reachable hospital. Then a money floor with a loan line and administration (`start-from-zero.md` B3).

### 4.3 Nothing to aim at after hour one (M to L)

*Why it matters.* CS1 ties unlocks to population milestones and keeps handing out buildings for 20 hours [1]; Anno's
tier ladder and expeditions, TF2's eras, Frostpunk's scenarios, Against the Storm's runs all give the player a next
thing [18][32][35][45]. Manor Lords, for all its sales, is scored around 71 by critics largely for having "no endgame"
[21][36]. Even the cosy end shows the two viable shapes: Townscaper sells on having no goals at all, Islanders on a
score [22]; Civitas is neither a toy nor a game yet.

*What exists.* 13 goals, advisor hints, the pulsing tech chip, 37 tech nodes. Goals end at "First bus line" around
minute 60-80 for a guided player (`early-game-audit.md` "When each goal completes"); the bots already showed idle gaps
of 10-16 minutes inside the first two hours. The demos have guided tours but no objectives. `start-from-zero.md`
designs ranks (hamlet → city), a Frontier start, appeal and prospects, and scenarios; none is built, and Rich has not
answered its questions.

*Fix.* Settlement ranks with charter grants and a celebration (start-from-zero C), on the live world as well as a
frontier; a milestone feed; three scenarios with medals on the demo pipeline (E). The tech tree as "a proper tree"
(UTD 65bf815a) is the UI half.

### 4.4 Problems are invisible and the data views are thin (M)

*Why it matters.* The entry-level contract of the genre is "the game tells you what is wrong and where": CS1's info
views and building icons, CS2's problem icons and Chirper (relentless, but a diagnostic), SC13's data layers, which
even its harshest reviews praised [1][12][27]. Anno 1800 was marked down for being poor at surfacing critical
information [32]. Civitas has excellent tooltips and panels but you must already know where to hover.

*What exists.* One overlay (congestion, H), the underground view (N), the world map, the charts panel, ledger, parcel
panel, and toasts/hints. Owner-reported defects on the board: click accuracy (96efc16d), park choice mouseover
(4aaac37e), build list with priorities (1a370951), popup bubbles for issues (88a8783f), flickering lights
(f91d8841). Every letter key is taken (`docs/charts.md`, `docs/transit.md`) and none can be remapped.

*Fix.* Issue bubbles over buildings and a problems list (no road, no builders, unstaffed, full, unhappy, no
customers); an overlays menu on one key (coverage, leisure, happiness, jobs/homes, later land value and pollution);
a tool palette that does not need the keyboard; the click-target fix.

### 4.5 The economy is a head count (L)

*Why it matters.* SC4 and CS1 both run on land value: it decides what grows where, what you can tax, and why a park
pays for itself [3][15]. Anno and W&R run on goods, and W&R's 92%-positive 1.0 shows how far players will follow a
logistics economy that is honest about cost [19]. Cities XXL was dismissed because wealth came too easily and
management was "rendered pointless" [43]. Without a price signal there is nothing to optimise, and nothing to trade.

*What exists.* Property tax `$40 × rate` per resident, wage tax to the workplace's owner, sales tax, fees, upkeep
(`early-game-audit.md` "The rules that matter"). Land is `LAND_PRICE_PER_TILE = 10` everywhere; no sale, rent, or
market; the world map's "book value" is cost, not price (`docs/world-map.md` §2). The model rewards zoning homes and
jobs barely pay (friction 7). The tax slider is the biggest lever and nothing says so.

*Fix.* "Derived fields: land value v1, pollution and noise as per-chunk bytes with overlays" (bbe84771), shipped like
the congestion bytes, feeding property tax, growth level and an overlay; a budget panel with service sliders and a
loan; then "Parcels: sell, subdivide and merge between players" (369e34eb) priced off it.

### 4.6 Roads look and behave like a prototype (M for classes, XL for curves)

*Why it matters.* Roads are the thing a city-builder player touches most. CS1's success was its road tools and the
Workshop that grew past 200,000 items largely around them [1][2]; CS2 made roads "the backbone" of its design and
shipped curves, parking and lane maths [10]; Manor Lords and Foundation sold on gridless organic streets [21][23].
Civitas has one class, no one-way, no avenues, no highways, no curves or diagonals, and a 4-tile grid look the owner
calls "dreadful" (UTD d08904bb). Traffic tuning also found the player's two tools do not work on this grid: signals
make the Commons slower (44% → 36% speed) and a ring road makes it worse (`docs/traffic-tuning.md`); two-thirds of
give-ups are trucks and visitors the player did not create.

*What exists.* The traffic *model* is the best in the field (`sim/traffic.ts`). Roundabouts and flyovers landed. The
aux byte has room for classes but only `0xfb`-`0xff` are free (`docs/underground.md` §1), so classes must be planned.

*Fix.* The road design fix first (kerbs, markings, junction geometry, the 4-tile block default), then "Roads:
classes in the aux layer" (26c5cb9a). Make visitor and freight load tunable so jams are the player's own doing.
Curves are a different ground model and belong in a later, separate card.

### 4.7 Audio: nothing (M)

*Why it matters.* CS1's ambient layers and radio, CS2's two radio networks with voiced anchors and weather-driven
ambience that follows the camera [8], SC13's praised soundscape, Townscaper's serene audio [22]: sound is how a city
feels alive when you are zoomed out, and silence reads as unfinished. There is no `AudioContext`, `Audio` element or
sound file anywhere in `packages/client/src`.

*Fix.* An ambient mix driven by what the client already knows (cars drawn, agents drawn, hour, zone under the
camera), the organic-variation rule applied to onset and pitch, vehicle sound in FPV, UI clicks, a mute. Web Audio
is cheap; licensed music later.

### 4.8 Utilities (M as coverage; XL as pipes)

*Why it matters.* Power, water and waste have been the baseline since SimCity 1989 and of CS1/CS2; W&R made them the
whole game [19]. Players read their absence as early access. They are also the natural inter-player contract in a
shared world, the SC4 neighbour deal made live [15].

*What exists.* Nothing. Waste depots run bin rounds with no waste model (`docs/mobile-jobs.md` §4). Cards:
"Utilities as coverage networks" (74d9e42e) and Rich's own "utilities" with water, gas, electric, internet, mobile
sub-cards (191430bc).

*Fix.* Coverage-model utilities with capacity, price per unit and cross-owner contracts (`feature-gaps.md` card 10).
No pipes.

### 4.9 Flat terrain and no map editor (XL for relief; M for rivers and a map tool)

*Why it matters.* Hills, rivers and coasts are why CS1 and TF2 maps are memorable [34]; they make bridges and land
value mean something. Civitas's world is flat and its water is noise lakes plus a hand-painted Thames. Height is
"visual only" even on bridges (`docs/structures.md` §3). "Terrain relief behind a world-version flag" (df1503a9) is
Backlog. Lower priority for an entry-level game than the eight items above.

### 4.10 The shared-world loop has no substance yet (L)

*Why it matters.* This is the strategic bet and the thing no competitor has. SC13's regions failed on always-online
DRM, dead servers and 2 km cities, not on the idea; shared Great Works, traded power and water and specialised cities
were the parts players liked, and EA still added an offline mode a year later [12][13][30][31]. Facebook's SimCity
Social and CityVille died within three years [44]; Eco's persistent planet works through laws and currencies, not
zoning [38]. Nobody has made the shared town fun yet.

*What exists.* Parcels and land rules are good. Taxes and fees do cross owners. But identity is a typed name with no
secret (`net.ts:206`: anyone who types "Rich" owns Rich's land), no chat or presence beyond "online N", no parcel
trading, anyone may bulldoze a public road (`net.ts:508`), and the live world is empty. The Commons is the only
neighbour, and it out-grows and out-jams the player.

*Fix (after the single-player loop is fun).* Accounts, parcel protection and a griefing policy, chat and presence, a
trade intent, per-town local demand (start-from-zero B), venues that draw across towns.

### Also behind, lower impact for a newcomer

- **Transit carries nobody** because `findJob` takes the nearest vacancy: ~0.4% of trips by bus, 3-11 rail riders a
  day, a river bus that "carried nobody" (`docs/transit.md`, `docs/demo.md`). A job-choice model (wage, skill,
  commute) is M and would also form districts.
- **Player content and modding** (M): the catalogue is data; "Bring your own building designs" (1148d027).
- **Districts and policies** (S-M): tax incentives (39ad64bb), noise limits, no-trucks.
- **Tourism and attractions** (M): visitors never stay or visit leisure places.
- **Crime** (M, after incidents). **Pollution and noise** (M, after derived fields).
- **Trams, shared track and rail signals** (M): one line per track today (`docs/rail.md`).

| Architecture lists tile, parcel, aux and sub layers (`README.md:145`) | a fifth, the under layer, exists (`docs/underground.md` §1) |
| Signals "roughly doubling what a busy junction can carry" (`tech.ts` tr2 blurb) | measured: the Commons runs faster *without* signals (`docs/traffic-tuning.md`) |

### 6.2 Half-finished systems

- **Education:** E1 and E2 are in; schooling, skill-gated jobs, wages, migration tilt, policies, charts and the London
  universities (E3-E8) are design, and `tech.ts` already shows `edu1`-`edu5` nodes whose buildings employ staff but
  teach nobody.
- **London landmarks:** placeable only in sandboxes; unlock rule undecided (`tech.ts` `lnd1.locked`).
- **Start from zero:** design only, five open questions for Rich (`docs/start-from-zero.md` §5).
- **Boats:** every subtask built, but the river bus carried nobody and "Boats: decisions for Rich" (UTD f88e06b8) is open.
- **Mobile jobs:** "about one rider trip in ten ends with the rider stuck"; bin rounds run out of crew
  (`docs/mobile-jobs.md` §9); tradespeople vans reserved, not built.
- **Local mode:** the static build still carries the WebSocket path and `/api` fetches; no `file://`; no update
  toast; no auto-pick-local (`docs/local-mode.md` "Not done").
- **Rail:** no signals, so no shared track; no track over water; one train per there-and-back line.
- **World map:** no parcel sale, no minimap, 3 s poll (`docs/world-map.md` §5).
- **Gateways:** "visible arrivals and departures" is still Up Next (8554984c).

### 6.3 Tuning and balance risks

- **Global demand.** `rDemand = jobs - population + 6` over the whole world (`simulation.ts:794`): a newcomer's growth
  depends on the Commons and on every other player; a remote plot never bootstraps (`start-from-zero.md` §1).
- **Tuned by bots, for two hours.** Every economy and tech number was fitted to a scripted bot on one plot shape
  (`early-game-audit.md`). No human playtest data is recorded; nothing after hour two is tuned.
- **The Commons jams the world.** Average speed 20-45% by day 5-6 regardless of what the player does; the jam alert
  fires daily from day 5.
- **Income scales with residents, not with play.** The optimal strategy is "zone homes"; tax tolerance 25% + coverage
  makes the slider a hidden lever.
- **One week = one year** makes a child a school-leaver 17 hours later (`docs/education.md` §11).
- **Transit mode share** under 1%: buses are a cost centre at every scale tested.
- **Visitors and freight** are two-thirds of give-ups and are not tunable by the player.

### 6.4 Scalability limits

- **One process, one world.** 60k citizens at 29 ms (harness) to 76 ms (browser test city) on an M2 Max; the 50 ms
  budget puts the ceiling around 60-100k citizens per world, no sharding (README roadmap).
- **Bandwidth per client grows with the city**: 11 KB/s at 1×1 to 193 KB/s at 8×8; parcels and players broadcast to
  everyone; agent frames are JSON (`load-tests.md` recommendation 3).
- **Saves are one JSON file**: 1.2 KB per citizen with transient mover state, 95 MB at 8×8, synchronous
  `JSON.stringify` every 30 s on the sim thread (`docs/local-mode.md` §3). No database.
- **Browser local mode** is 3-10× slower than Node: a 3×3 city averaged 135 ms a tick in a headless worker, nearly
  three times the budget; phones untested. For an entry-level player local mode must own 1×1-2×2 comfortably, and
  that has not been measured on an ordinary laptop in the foreground.
- **Aux byte space** is nearly spent (`0xfb`-`0xff` free). **Subscriptions** cap at 256 chunks per client.

### 6.5 Audio, accessibility, input

- No audio (§4.7). About 20 `aria-` attributes, one `prefers-reduced-motion` check in `viewer.ts` only, 11 px help
  text, congestion by colour alone, no UI scale, no key remap. CS2 at least ships remappable keys and a resizable UI,
  and was criticised for lacking a colourblind mode [41]; Highrise City was marked down for little accessibility [42].
- Touch: five pointer/touch references, one 700 px media query; desktop-only in practice although it ships as a PWA.
- No localisation layer.

### 6.6 Save, load and versioning

- Snapshot `VERSION = 8`, in-place upgrades from 3-7 (`server/src/snapshot.ts`); an unreadable file is renamed
  `.vN.bak` and a *new world starts*: a silent reset for a downgraded server.
- `local:check` and `history:check` cover round trips, but there is no fixture corpus of old saves, no CI
  (`.github/` does not exist), no unit-test framework; checks are hand-run scripts.
- Transient fields are saved and then reset on load, so a reload empties every road.
- Local mode: IndexedDB, Safari seven-day eviction, quota toasts. `data/world.json` is written whole every 30 s.

### 6.7 Multiplayer griefing and fairness

- **Identity:** `hello` lower-cases the name into an id; no password, token or cookie (`net.ts:206-214`). Anyone can
  play as anyone. This alone makes a public shared world unsafe.
- **Roads:** public roads on unclaimed land can be bulldozed by anyone (`net.ts:508`); roads on unclaimed land can
  shape access and block parcels.
- **Land:** adjacency and no-islanding rules are good; `MAX_PARCEL_EDGE = 48` and $10 a tile mean a day-one player
  can claim 2,000 tiles and fence a region.
- **Sandboxes:** `mayEdit` returns true for anyone (`net.ts:360`); shared demos can be wrecked by any visitor.
- **Rate limits:** none found on intents, subscriptions or `linePreview` beyond its 100 ms cadence.
- **Fairness:** the Commons has every technology and a head start; wage tax to the workplace owner means a late
  joiner who zones offices next to your homes takes your residents' tax.
- **Moderation:** no reporting, kick, ban or admin tools beyond env vars and the RESET file.

## 7. Where Civitas is ahead, and how to lean into it

1. **The readable life simulation.** Make it *matter* and *visible*: incidents, schools, jobs and land value land on
   named people and the game says so ("Priya lost her job when the factory closed"). The citizen panel and history
   rings are the surface; a "day in the life" button on the goals card is the entry-level hook.
2. **Traffic that is actually simulated.** Give it road classes and a traffic info view (turn counts, origins) that
   CS players pay mods for, and make jams the player's own doing.
3. **Transport breadth and cab rides.** Make commutes long enough to need it (job choice), and build scenario maps
   that are transport puzzles (Railhead, Fishing village).
4. **Pure-function vehicles.** Zero per-vehicle wire is why the browser client scales; keep it for trams and cyclists.
5. **Browser, offline, spectator.** The London showcase is a trailer in a tab. Make local mode the newcomer's first
   session (instant, pausable, 4×) and the shared world the second; share links to a view (`DEFAULT_VIEW` exists).
6. **The shared world with land rules.** After the single-player loop is fun: accounts, trade, chat. A town of five
   friends is the demo that sells the thesis.
7. **The build machinery.** Publish the catalogue format and viewer as the modding story; add a human playtest log
   next to the bot runs.
8. **The organic-variation rule.** It is why the city reads as alive at night. Apply it to audio and incidents.
